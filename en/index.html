03 · ai-workflow

How I work with AI

AI is not autocomplete — it is a workflow you can engineer. This is what I actually do.

  • Spec-driven: every project keeps a SPEC.md / ROADMAP.md as its single source of truth. AI implements against the spec; a human signs off. Writing down what you want costs less than correcting the output ten times.
  • Safety-boundary design: the LLM may only pick a tool from an allowlist and fill its arguments — it never executes raw text. The escape hatch is off by default, and still blocklisted once enabled. The boundary lives in code, not in a prompt.
  • Verifiable output: doc-pipeline ships an eval set — structural completeness checked deterministically in code, semantic quality scored by an LLM judge against a rubric. Automation only holds when verification costs less than production.
  • Knowledge as engineering artefacts: team documentation standards, design-doc templates, and test-data conversion become version-controlled skills — onboarding stops being oral tradition and becomes something you can diff and roll back.

04 · quality

Quality & testing

  • Testing as an institution: authored the unit-test review checklist for a bank's credit-authorization system, iterated across six versions as the team's practice matured — a testing standard is a living document, not a one-off deliverable.
  • One source of truth for test data: Markdown tables are the source of truth; a tool generates the xlsx the tests actually read — so test data can be reviewed and version-controlled.
  • E2E and accessibility: wrote a reusable accessibility testing standard and review checklist (data-testid locator strategy, ARIA rules), applied with Playwright and axe.
  • Safe by default: appup is read-only unless you explicitly confirm an upgrade; voice-mac-agent keeps dangerous operations off by default. A tool's defaults are its character.
